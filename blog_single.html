How to read a construction bid without getting burned

RARafiq Ahmed, Head of Estimating12 Oct 20266 min read

Two bids for the same building can sit 20 percent apart, and neither contractor is lying. The gap usually hides in what each bid includes, what it assumes, and what it leaves for you to discover later. Here are the five places we check first.

1. The scope of work

Compare scope line by line before you compare totals. A low bid that leaves out site clearing, temporary power or external works is a variation waiting to happen. Ask each bidder to list exclusions in plain words.

2. Allowances and provisional sums

An allowance is a guess with a price on it. Tiles, light fittings and ground conditions are common ones. Ask what the allowance covers, who decides the final choice, and who pays when the real cost is higher.

The cheapest bid is rarely the lowest cost. The lowest cost is the bid with the fewest assumptions.

3. Schedule and its assumptions

A short programme is only useful if it is believable. Look for the monsoon, permit approvals and material lead times. If the schedule ignores them, the dates will not hold.

4. Payment terms

Payments should follow finished work, not the calendar. Milestones tied to inspected progress keep both sides honest and protect your cash flow.

5. Warranty and handover

Check how long the warranty runs, what it covers and how fast the contractor must respond. At Stonebridge, structure and waterproofing carry ten years, and we hand over with full as-built drawings.
